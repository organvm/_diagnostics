# Scope — Ideal machinations of a diagnostics system

## Core Inquiry

> **What invariant mechanisms must any system possess to faithfully know the live state of another system it cannot fully observe — and to act on that knowledge without corrupting either the observed system or its own judgment?**

The grounding referent is `memdiag` (built this session): a closed read-only loop
SENSE → STRATIFY → RECONCILE → LOCATE → CLOSE, born from a crisis that had to be
reconstructed 48h late. But the inquiry is for the **ideal form**, of which memdiag is
one instance — the question is what *any* faithful diagnostics system must do.

## Essential vs. Peripheral

**Essential (must explore):**
- **Epistemic faithfulness under decay** — true state is knowable only through signals that age, lie, and resize under you. The core problem is not measurement but *trust*.
- **Non-perturbation** — to observe is, physically, to consume. A monitor that uses the scarce resource it measures becomes the disease. Is non-perturbing diagnosis even possible, or only asymptotically approachable?
- **Loop-closure** — a diagnosis that terminates in a report is half a machine. Knowledge must feed action *and* re-observation.
- **Contributor vs. cause** — the discipline of not killing the loudest process.
- **Reconciliation as the primary act** — the delta between believed-state and live-state *is* the finding.

**Peripheral (can defer):**
- The specific signal domain (memory vs. CPU vs. disk vs. network).
- Implementation substrate (bash, language, UI, the specific 16 GB host).
- The catalog of particular findings (FIND-001…010 are instances, not the ideal).

## Hidden Ambiguities

1. **Agency.** "Machinations" connotes scheming. Is the ideal diagnostics system a passive instrument, or an active investigator that forms and tests hypotheses? The lenses should not assume passivity.
2. **Ideal *for whom?*** For the operator (legibility), for the diagnosed system (minimal disturbance), or for the future (recurrence-prevention)? These optimize differently. The deepest reading: **an ideal diagnostics system works to make itself unnecessary.**
3. **Boundary confusion.** Diagnosis blurs into monitoring (continuous), prognosis (forward), and remediation (mutation). Where does diagnosis *end*? The non-perturbation constraint argues remediation is a *different machine*.
4. **The observer paradox at the limit.** If every observation perturbs, the perfectly faithful diagnostics system is impossible in principle — only approachable. The "ideal" may be a limit, not an attainable state.
