# Logic — Ideal machinations of a diagnostics system

The Scope lens fixed the core inquiry: *what invariant mechanisms must any system possess to faithfully know the live state of another system it cannot fully observe, and act on that knowledge without corrupting either the observed system or its own judgment?* This lens does not enumerate features. It treats each candidate mechanism as a **claim** and asks whether the claim survives a why → how → what-if descent. A mechanism that survives three levels of interrogation without contradicting itself is invariant; one that collapses at level two was a convenience, not an invariant.

The grounding instance is `memdiag` — a read-only `SENSE → STRATIFY → RECONCILE → LOCATE → CLOSE` loop with one trustworthy headline metric (memory-free %, after swap-% was demoted for resizing under the observer) and a `localized`-vs-`cumulative` discriminator. I use it as a worked theorem, not as a topic.

## Five Lines

The five orthodox lines are chosen so that each isolates one *necessary condition* for faithful knowledge-and-action across an observability gap. They are not five "areas"; they are five places where the ideal can fail, ordered roughly from epistemics inward to teleology:

1. **The Faithfulness Predicate** — what must `believed-state ⊨ live-state` formally mean, and what is the maximal faithfulness achievable when the channel decays?
2. **The Observer-Perturbation Bound** — if observing consumes the measured resource, is non-perturbing diagnosis possible even in principle, or only asymptotically?
3. **The Trust-Ordering of Signals** — by what calculus does one signal outrank another, given that the loudest signal is often the least reliable (swap-% vs. free-%)?
4. **The Halting Condition of a Diagnosis** — when is a diagnosis *done*, given that "found the cause" and "found a contributor" are different stopping rules?
5. **Self-Abolition as Objective Function** — is "an ideal diagnostics system works to make itself unnecessary" a coherent optimization target, or a category error?

## Recursive Tree

### Line 1 — The Faithfulness Predicate

- **L1 (Why must faithfulness be defined as a *relation*, not a *property*?)** Because "the report is accurate" is meaningless without a referent: accurate *with respect to what, when*. Faithfulness is the relation `R(B, S, t)` between the believed-state `B` the system holds and the live-state `S` at observation time `t`. The 2026-05-21 autopsy nearly issued kills against processes reaped 48h earlier — proof that `B` referenced `S(t₀)` while action targeted `S(t₁)`. The relation, not the report, is the unit of correctness.
  - **L2 (How is the relation made checkable, given `S` is never fully observable?)** It cannot be checked against `S` directly — only against a *later, fresher observation* of `S`. So faithfulness is operationalized as **reconciliation**: `R` holds iff a re-observation `S(t₁)` does not contradict the action implied by `B(t₀)`. This is exactly memdiag's `reconcile` subcommand — the delta table *is* the faithfulness test, and "the delta IS the finding" is the predicate made executable. Faithfulness becomes a *temporal* property: not "is `B` true" but "does `B` survive contact with a newer `S`."
    - **L3 (What if no fresh `S(t₁)` is obtainable before action is forced?)** Then faithfulness degrades to a **bound on staleness**, and the only honest move is to attach a confidence-decay to every belief: `R` is not binary but a function of `Δt = t₁ − t₀` against the observed system's volatility. memdiag encodes this implicitly (snapshots are timestamped; the verdict reads "the trustworthy headline" of *this* snapshot). The ideal form generalizes it: **every belief carries an expiry derived from the measured volatility of the signal that produced it.** A faithfulness predicate without a staleness bound is a lie with good intentions — it asserts `R(B,S,t₀)` as if it were `R(B,S,now)`.

### Line 2 — The Observer-Perturbation Bound

- **L1 (Why is perturbation not merely an engineering nuisance but a logical limit?)** Because the diagnostic apparatus is *inside* the resource economy it measures. A memory diagnostic allocates memory to run; a CPU profiler burns cycles; reading `/proc` faults pages. The Scope lens named the disease precisely: "a monitor consuming the resource it measures becomes the disease." This is not removable by better engineering — it is structural, the macroscopic analogue of measurement back-action.
  - **L2 (How does one minimize, rather than eliminate, the perturbation?)** By making the observation's resource footprint *strictly smaller than the resolution it needs to detect*, and by refusing residence. memdiag's two design choices are exactly these: (a) **no daemon, no LaunchAgent** — the observer does not co-reside with the patient between episodes, so its steady-state perturbation is *zero*; it perturbs only during the sub-second `snap`; (b) `watch` is "sub-millisecond... spawns nothing that outlives the call." The principle: **perturbation is acceptable iff it is impulsive and amortizes to zero, never continuous.** A continuous monitor pays rent forever; an on-demand probe pays a one-time toll.
    - **L3 (What if the act of *not* residing means you miss the transient you needed to catch?)** This is the bound stated sharply: you cannot simultaneously have (i) zero steady-state perturbation and (ii) guaranteed capture of an unannounced transient. The two are conjugate. The ideal resolves it not by choosing one but by **externalizing the trigger**: let the *patient's own cheap signals* (a shell-prompt hook calling `watch`, costing sub-ms) decide *when* to spend the expensive observation. The observer stays dormant; the environment wakes it at the threshold. Non-perturbing continuous diagnosis is impossible in principle — but **threshold-triggered impulsive diagnosis** asymptotically approaches it, paying perturbation only in the neighborhood of the event that justifies it. The limit is real; the approach is the design.

### Line 3 — The Trust-Ordering of Signals

- **L1 (Why must signals be *ranked* rather than *fused*?)** Because naive fusion (averaging, voting) assumes signals are independent and comparably reliable. They are not. Some signals are *adversarial to the observer*: swap-% "proved unstable — the OS resizes swap under you," so the denominator of the ratio mutates between reads. A fused metric that includes a signal whose definition changes mid-measurement inherits that instability and *launders it as confidence*. Ranking lets you quarantine the liar; fusion lets the liar vote.
  - **L2 (How is the rank order derived — what makes one signal outrank another?)** By a two-axis test: **stability of definition** (does the signal mean the same thing across reads?) and **reclaimability-honesty** (does the number reflect actually-committed state?). memdiag's choices fall out of this calculus: free-% beats swap-% (stable denominator vs. resizing one); free-% beats "pages free" because "inactive pages are reclaimable" — pages-free *overcounts scarcity*, reporting pressure that the OS would relieve on demand. The rank is therefore: **a signal that is stable-in-definition and honest-about-reclaimability outranks a signal that is louder but mutable or misleading.** Loudness is anti-correlated with trust here; the alarming signal (swap thrash, low pages-free) is precisely the untrustworthy one.
    - **L3 (What if the highest-trust signal is also the least *sensitive* — it stays calm while the system dies?)** This is the real cost of trust-ranking: you may demote the only signal that moves early. The resolution is **stratification, not suppression** — keep the low-trust signals visible but *typed*. memdiag does exactly this: it prints swap as "(unstable signal — informational)" rather than deleting it. The untrustworthy signal is retained as a *hypothesis-generator* (it may flag *where* to look) but is barred from being the *headline verdict* (it may not decide *what is true*). The ideal form: **trust-ordering governs which signal may close a verdict, not which signal may be seen.** A signal can be admissible as evidence-of-direction while inadmissible as evidence-of-state. Demoting swap to informational, not deleting it, is the difference between a calibrated instrument and a blind one.

### Line 4 — The Halting Condition of a Diagnosis

- **L1 (Why is "found the cause" the wrong default halting condition?)** Because it presumes a cause exists to be found. The 2026-05-21 mode was "cumulative co-residency — no single process dominates": the failure had *no single bug*. A diagnosis that halts only on finding a dominant cause will **never terminate** in the additive-failure case, or worse, will manufacture a false culprit to satisfy its own stopping rule (kill the loudest process — the exact error the autopsy nearly committed). The halting condition must be defined over the *shape of the evidence*, not over the existence of a villain.
  - **L2 (How does the ideal distinguish "localized" from "cumulative," and halt correctly in each?)** By a concentration test on contribution. memdiag computes it explicitly: if the top process holds `> 40%` of used memory, the shape is `localized` and the halting condition is "name the dominant contributor"; otherwise it is `cumulative co-residency` and the halting condition is *different* — "no single process dominates" is itself the terminal finding. The deep move is that **`cumulative` is a valid, complete diagnosis, not a failure to diagnose.** The system halts on a *characterization of the distribution* (concentrated vs. diffuse), and each characterization licenses a different action (excise-one vs. budget-all).
    - **L3 (What if the concentration threshold itself is the artifact — 40% is arbitrary, and reality is a spectrum?)** Then the halting condition must report not a verdict but a *confidence-typed verdict*, and the threshold becomes a **declared, falsifiable parameter** rather than hidden truth. memdiag hard-codes 40% — adequate for an instance, insufficient for the ideal. The ideal generalizes: halt when the evidence's *shape is stable under re-observation* (reconcile twice; if the localized/cumulative classification flips between snapshots, you have not halted — you are sampling noise). Termination is thus **second-order**: a diagnosis halts not when it finds an answer but when the *answer stops changing across fresh observations*. This re-couples Line 4 to Line 1 — the halting condition is itself a faithfulness test applied to the verdict rather than to the raw state. A diagnosis is done when its own conclusion has become stale-resistant.

### Line 5 — Self-Abolition as Objective Function

- **L1 (Why entertain "make itself unnecessary" as a goal at all — isn't a permanent instrument better?)** Because a diagnostics system that optimizes for its own continued use has an incentive structure inverted against the patient: its utility rises with the patient's sickness. The Scope lens's "deepest reading" — the ideal works to make itself unnecessary — is an attempt to align the observer's objective with the *health* of the observed, not with its own engagement. The question is whether that target is coherent or self-defeating.
  - **L2 (How could a system act so as to reduce future demand for itself?)** By converting each diagnosis into a *durable structural change* in the patient or its operator that removes the recurrence — i.e., closing the loop into prevention. memdiag's `close` subcommand is exactly this attempt: it emits not just an IRF row but a **behavioral forcing function** ("treat 16GB as a budget, not a floor"; "one heavy AI surface at a time"). The diagnosis tries to die into a *habit* that prevents the next episode. Self-abolition is operationalized as **every finding must propose the structural change that would make this finding impossible next time.** A report that does not is, by this objective, incomplete.
    - **L3 (What if self-abolition is incoherent — the system can never fully succeed, because succeeding would destroy the instrument that detects whether it succeeded?)** Here is the genuine paradox. If the system perfectly prevents all recurrence, it removes its own evidence-source and can no longer verify that prevention holds — it must keep observing to know it is unneeded, which means it is still needed *as a verifier*. So "make itself unnecessary" cannot mean "cease to exist"; it can only mean **migrate from active-investigator to dormant-verifier**: reduce its own *duty cycle* toward zero while preserving its *readiness* at zero steady-state cost. This is precisely why memdiag is on-demand-only with a sub-ms `watch`: the ideal end-state is not deletion but **a dormant probe that costs nothing until the prevention it installed fails.** Self-abolition is coherent only as *asymptotic duty-cycle minimization*, never as termination. The objective function is "minimize my own activation frequency subject to retaining the power to detect my own obsolescence's breach" — and that fixed point is dormancy, not death. This closes back onto Line 2: zero-steady-state-perturbation and self-abolition are the same limit viewed from epistemics and from teleology respectively.

## Strongest Branch

**The load-bearing branch is Line 1 → L2 → L3: faithfulness, operationalized as reconciliation against a fresher observation, degrading gracefully to a *staleness bound* when no fresh observation is available.**

It is strongest for four convergent reasons:

1. **It is the only branch the other four reduce to.** Line 4's deepest level (halt when the verdict is "stale-resistant") is Line 1 applied to the conclusion. Line 5's paradox dissolves into "dormant *verifier*" — a faithfulness-checker that must keep confirming `R` holds. Line 3's admissibility rule ("a signal may decide a verdict only if its definition is stable across reads") is a faithfulness condition on signals. Line 2's impulse-triggering exists to *acquire the fresh `S(t₁)`* that reconciliation demands. Every other invariant is a corollary of "compare belief to a newer reading of reality." This is the genus; the rest are species.

2. **It is the named failure mode of the founding incident.** The autopsy "nearly issued kills against processes reaped 48h earlier." That is exactly `R(B, S, t₀)` mistaken for `R(B, S, now)` — the staleness bound absent. The branch is not theoretical; it is the post-mortem of the crisis that produced the referent.

3. **It is the most violated invariant in real systems.** Dashboards, alerting, and reports overwhelmingly assert *present truth* from *past measurement* with no attached expiry. Most monitoring is faithful at `t₀` and silently unfaithful at every moment after. The branch names the single discipline whose absence is nearly universal: **a belief without an expiry is a defect, regardless of how true it was when minted.**

4. **It survived its own descent without contradiction.** Pushed three levels, it did not collapse into a convenience — it *strengthened*, terminating in a constructive bound (confidence-decay = `f(Δt, volatility)`) rather than a paradox. By the lens's own test — survive why → how → what-if without self-contradiction — this is the branch that demonstrably holds, and the only one that re-derives the others.

The one-line theorem: **faithful diagnosis is not the production of true reports but the maintenance of a relation between belief and a continuously-refreshed observation, such that no belief is allowed to outlive the volatility of the signal that produced it.**
